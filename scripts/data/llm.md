# scripts/data

## Purpose
Reference data that a script writes or reads and that is committed, so a change to it is a diff someone
can review. Delete this folder and the vehicle finder has no Year / Make / Model list to load.

## Owns
- [`vehicles.csv`](vehicles.csv) — every make and model the finder offers, with the model years it was
  built. Written by [`../vehicles-fetch.mjs`](../vehicles-fetch.mjs) from NHTSA vPIC; 47 makes, 843
  models, model years 2005–2027 as fetched 2026-09-30. Columns: `make`, `model`, `years` (ranges
  joined by `;`, e.g. `2005-2011;2019-2027`), `types` (vPIC's `car` / `mpv` / `truck`, joined by `;`).

- [`vehicle-fitment.csv`](vehicle-fitment.csv) — real wheel fitment, one row per make + model + model
  year that the ANKUSI wheel fitment dataset (release 2026.10.04) publishes for **that exact year**,
  keyed on `vehicles.csv`'s spelling. Written by [`../vehicle-fitment.mjs`](../vehicle-fitment.mjs)
  (**D149**, LA-05 of the D145 audit): 1,224 model-years, 463 models, 42 makes. Columns: `make`,
  `model`, `year`, `bolt_pattern`, `centre_bore_mm`, `lug_count`, `oe_wheel_diameter`,
  `oe_wheel_width`, `oe_offset_mm` (blank unless one same-front-and-rear factory size is published),
  `source_model` (the ANKUSI name or names the row came from). No tire size: ANKUSI gives one on 430
  of 9,701 rows, nearly all other markets' cars, and the owner kept the D48(4) placeholder for tires.

- [`vehicle-fitment-generations.csv`](vehicle-fitment-generations.csv) — **hand-kept, never written by a
  script** (**D149**, LA-05; owner, 2026-10-05: *"Do a market search and vendor search to fill
  whatever is poss"*). One row per make + model + generation year range, for the years the exact-year file
  does not cover. Columns: `make`, `model` (vehicles.csv spelling), `year_from`, `year_to`, `bolt_pattern`
  (`5x114.3`, or `unknown`), `centre_bore_mm` (blank unless the two sources agree), `source_1`,
  `source_2` (full page URLs). 1,048 rows: 972 real rows on 595 models and 76 `unknown` rows. Resolution
  of the 7,528 offered model-years (2026-10-05): 1,224 exact, 3,979 generation, 1,656 nearest, 167 none
  (inside an `unknown` range), 502 with no real row and no placeholder either (no vehicle data), 0 placeholder.

- [`vehicle-fitment-autocountry.csv`](vehicle-fitment-autocountry.csv) — **generated** by
  [`../vehicle-fitment-autocountry.mjs`](../vehicle-fitment-autocountry.mjs) (**D155**) from the saved answers of
  Auto Country's vehicle lookup. One row per Auto Country make + model + model year, in **Auto Country's own
  spelling**; lists are joined with `;`. Columns: `make`, `model`, `year`, `trims`, `bodies`, `bolt_patterns`
  (every distinct pattern the trims state; two = trims differ), `centre_bore`, `thread` (`12x1.5`, or
  `1/2x20` for UNF), `fastener` (`nut` / `bolt`), `offset` (these four: the most common value across trims,
  a tie takes the larger bore), `tire_sizes` and `wheel_sizes` (union over every trim, body and axle; a
  staggered option contributes both axles; flotation kept as `33x12.50R15`; `M+S` and `P`/`LT` stripped),
  `tire_dia_min`/`max` (overall inches), `tire_width_min`/`max` (mm), `wheel_dia_*`, `wheel_width_*`,
  `offset_*` (the `oem` tier of vehicle-ranges at stock height, union over bodies and axles, 999 dropped),
  and a trailing `classes` (`car`/`mpv`/`truck` from the body styles; an addition to the column list the
  contract fixed, used for the finder's tire-height class of an Auto Country-only model) and `routed_from`
  (trim routing, derived: a row named after one of OUR models that holds the trims of an Auto Country
  parent model, e.g. `Malibu: Classic LS`; those trims are left out of the parent's row, so a plain Civic
  keeps one bolt pattern and a "Civic Type R" row carries its own) and, last, **`tpms`** (**D156**: `on`,
  `off` or blank; `on` when any trim of the year says on, `off` for a year before 2008 where no trim does,
  blank otherwise: 9,403 on, 132 off, 130 blank of 9,665 rows). **Auto Country never says `off`** (its
  `chassis.tpms` is `"on"` or null in all 31,974 answers), so the pre-2008 `off` is *derived* and vetoable
  (`TPMS_ABSENT_MEANS_OFF_BEFORE` in the generator). Owner, 2026-10-06: *"for TM or TP MS frequency if auto
  country is giving on and off for car we can do the same and not focus on the frequency that is out of our
  scope"*. A model listed by
  Auto Country with no answer yet keeps a row with blank fit columns.
- [`vehicle-fitment-vendors.csv`](vehicle-fitment-vendors.csv) — gap fills (D155), generated by
  `../vehicle-fitment-merge-gaps.mjs`; see "Sourcing rules" below. 406 rows on 338 model-years in the first
  merge (2026-10-06): fields won d 330, p 239, m 15, s 139, w 705.
- [`vehicle-aliases.csv`](vehicle-aliases.csv) — **generated** by the same script. Columns: `make`, `model`,
  `year_from`, `year_to`, `canonical_make`, `canonical_model`, `relation`, `note`. `make`/`model` is the name
  the row is about, `canonical_*` the entry that carries its fit. Relations (owner, 2026-10-06, verbatim:
  *"union of both is best. dont lose data."* and *"One car, one entry: where both lists have the same car
  under two names, Auto Country's name wins and ours becomes an alias so old URLs still resolve. Nothing
  offered today disappears."*; the rules below are **derived** from it, vetoable):
  - `same`: one Auto Country model and one of ours are one car spelled differently (case, spaces,
    punctuation, `&`/`and`, a make prefix, or the script's `SAME` table). `model` = ours, `canonical_model` =
    Auto Country's name. Two cars whose bolt patterns differ that year are never `same`; an identical
    spelling is always one entry. A year of ours Auto Country does not list takes the nearest year's name.
    Two of ours can share one Auto Country name through the hand table (MINI `Hardtop` and `Cooper
    Convertible` -> `Cooper`; VW `Jetta Wagon`, `SportWagen`, `GLI` -> `Jetta`; derived), and a second Auto
    Country spelling or name of an already matched model of ours is a `member` alias of the first (Auto Country
    spells Nissan `TITAN` and `Titan`, Rogue Sport beside Qashqai). Auto Country's name wins, **except for
    the eight bare names of the `DISPLAY` table (D156, below)**: Tesla is `Model 3`/`Model S`/`Model X`/`Model
    Y` and Mazda `Mazda2`/`Mazda3`/`Mazda5`/`Mazda6` in the list, and Auto Country's bare spelling is the
    alias (`same`, note starts `display table (D156)`).
  - `member`: several Auto Country models are variants of one of ours and share one bolt pattern (Mercedes
    `C230` -> `C-Class`, `Suburban 1500` -> `Suburban`, and the minority spelling when Auto Country spells a
    model two ways over the years). Our name stays the entry; `model` = Auto Country's, an alias.
  - `split`: the variants' bolt patterns differ (`Express 1500` vs `2500`, `Silverado 1500` vs `1500 HD`):
    each is an entry of its own AND our family name stays an entry whose fit is the union of them.
  - `ac-only`: an Auto Country model with no counterpart, added under its name (exotics and medium-duty
    included). `ours-only`: one of ours Auto Country does not list, unchanged.
  - Make spelling stays ours (`MINI`, `Infiniti`); only models follow Auto Country. `VEHICLE_ALIASES` in the
    app takes `same` and `member` names only, drops any name that is itself an entry, and never chains.

- [`vehicle-exclusions.csv`](vehicle-exclusions.csv) — **hand-kept** (D156). Columns `make`, `model`, `year`,
  `reason`, `source`; `make`/`model` is the finder entry (canonical name), one row per model year the finder
  does **not** offer because the car was not sold in North America that year (25 rows: GMC Canyon 2013-14,
  Maserati GranTurismo 2020-22, VinFast VF 6 2023, 2026-27, VF 7 2023-25, VF9 2022, Ford GT MKII 2023-27, Honda
  FCX Clarity 2005-07, BMW M2 2022, Chrysler Cirrus 2006, Mitsubishi Diamante 2005, Chevrolet Suburban HD
  2026-27). Owner, 2026-10-06, verbatim: *"we can drop the data which does not match our catalog so those we
  can remove"*. `scripts/vehicle-fitment.mjs` applies it when it writes `vehicle-list.ts`: the year leaves
  the entry, and an entry left with no year (Cirrus, GT MKII, Diamante) leaves the list with its aliases, so a
  removed year is also never a redirect target in `canonical.ts`. **Only rows whose gap-fill note says "no NA
  car" and whose year carries no fit data from any source.** The script refuses (names every offender) a row
  for a year that Auto Country or a gap-fill row states a fact for: Chevrolet Suburban HD 2024-25 and VinFast
  VF 6 2024-25 carry a PRO WHEELS fill, so they stay (*derived*: a vendor sells wheels for them, and the
  rule is "no data and no NA car"; vetoable). `reason` is the gap-fill note, `source` the sheet it is in
  (`design/reports/fitment-research/gap-fill-2026-10-06/gapfill-N.csv`). The two `vehicle-gapfill-empty.csv`
  rows left in the finder are not NA cars, only blank: Porsche 918 2015 (centre-lock) and Spyker C12 2008.
- [`vehicle-added-patterns.csv`](vehicle-added-patterns.csv) — **hand-kept** (D156). Columns `make`, `model`,
  `year_from`, `year_to`, `bolt_pattern`, `reason`, `source` (a URL or a named report): a bolt pattern a car
  is known to have beyond what Auto Country states. It only **adds** (appended after Auto Country's own, never
  replacing or removing one) and only to a year that already has an Auto Country or gap-fill row; a bad pattern,
  a missing source or a year with no row stops the build. One row: **Toyota Tacoma 2015 gets `5x114.3`** beside
  Auto Country's `6x139.7` (the base 2WD four-cylinder, bore 60.1; Auto Country states both for 2005-14).
  Owner, 2026-10-06: *"take a decision yourself, whatever you feel right"* (*derived*, vetoable). Sources:
  wheel-size.com `/pcd/5x114.3/toyota/tacoma/1995-2015/`, sdwheel.com and thewheelshop.ca (all via
  `design/reports/fitment-research/phase7-market-check.md` #3). The year keeps Auto Country's single bore
  (106.1, the 6-lug hub), as 2005-14 do, so 5x114.3 wheels show under Similar there.

## Sourcing rules (D155, 2026-10-06; they replace the older two-source rule for new facts)
- **Order of trust: Auto Country first** (its lookup is the shop's primary supplier; owner: *"Yes, we can use
  AutoPrintly because that is our primary supplier and source of truth."*), **then the other vendors' public
  lookups (`d` Direct Auto Import, `p` PRO WHEELS), the car maker (`m`), our own committed sheets (`s`), any
  other website (`w`)**. Where Auto Country disagrees with anything else, Auto Country wins.
- **One source is enough** (owner: *"A value with one source only: load it directly."*). A row's `source`
  carries a trailing `1` when one source supports it (`w1`); two agreeing sources have no `1`. At equal
  letter a two-source row beats a single one.
- **The ban on Wheel-Size-derived sites is lifted** (`wheel-size.com`, `wheel-sizes.com`, `tirerack.com`,
  `tiresize.com`, `teoalida.com`, `faultpedia.com`, `tirewheelguide.com`): owner, 2026-10-06, *"we have
  permisiion to view and use data from them so go ahead"*. Recorded as the owner's statement; no document is
  on file. `BANNED_SOURCES` in `vehicle-fitment.mjs` is empty; add a host to refuse it again.
- Gap fills live in [`vehicle-fitment-vendors.csv`](vehicle-fitment-vendors.csv), **generated** from the
  `gapfill-*.csv` sheets by [`../vehicle-fitment-merge-gaps.mjs`](../vehicle-fitment-merge-gaps.mjs)
  (`node scripts/vehicle-fitment-merge-gaps.mjs <folder>`; per make + model + year and per field the highest
  trust wins; `;`-joined bore, thread and fastener keep their first value; every bolt pattern, tire size
  and wheel size stays). Columns: the Auto Country ones that matter (`bolt_patterns`, `centre_bore`, `thread`,
  `fastener`, `offset`, `tire_sizes`, `wheel_sizes`) plus `source`, `source_url`, `source_url_2`, `note`
  (provenance; the generator ignores the last three). Names are the finder's canonical spelling or an alias.
  `vehicle-fitment.mjs` only fills a field Auto Country left blank, or stands in for a year Auto Country lacks
  (record source = the letters used, `w1` for one single-source row); it prints how many of each by letter.
  Rows that state no fact (`no NA car`) are listed in `import-report/vehicle-gapfill-empty.csv`, conflicts
  between sources in `vehicle-gapfill-conflicts.csv`.
- Junk from Auto Country is blanked, not loaded, and listed with its raw value in
  `import-report/vehicle-fitment-junk.csv`: a bolt pattern with ten or more lugs (`12x112`, `15x130`;
  `10x225` and `10x335` dually patterns stay), a bore of 0, a thread that is not metric or UNF (`Peg`). A
  19.5 / 17.5 / 16.5 inch commercial wheel Auto Country writes as `19x6` takes the rim of its `R19.5` tire.

## Hand-kept sheets: sourcing rule (the older two-source rule, still enforced on these three files)
- A generation row goes in only when **two independent sources on two different websites** both name the
  vehicle and year range and agree on the bolt pattern. Disagreement or one source: no row. The sites that
  were banned (above) may now be one of the two; `autofiles.com`, `bolt-patterns.com` and `wheeloffset.net`
  share a template and errors and still count as one site.
- Not proven independent: `autofiles.com` and `boltpatternhq.com` agree almost everywhere, and the latter's
  About page says its records come partly from "historical wheel-size datasets". Owner, 2026-10-05: *"Yes,
  keep it (Recommended)"*: boltpatternhq counts as one of the two sources. `carsandcollectibles.com`
  (35 group-E rows) is accepted too (derived: agrees on 1,170 model-years, none contradicted).
  Known errors in them: boltpatternhq has Accent 2013+, Rio 2018+, Charger and Challenger on 5x114.3 and
  autofiles has Viper on 6x114.3; both are why a row needs agreement, not one site.
- A `bolt_pattern` of `unknown` marks years nobody should claim: it blocks the nearest-year rule and the
  placeholder there. It needs `source_1` (a page that shows why) and no `source_2`. 76 rows, from three
  rules: a gap between two real years whose patterns differ (Outback 2014, Ram 1500 2019-2024), a
  single-source dissent against what nearest-year would give (BMW 5/7-series 2017-2019, Mach-E 2023-2024),
  and by hand (Bronco 2023-2027: Raptor is 6x135; Charger 2024-2027: new platform).
- **Precedence in the app:** exact-year ANKUSI row, then generation row, then the **nearest real year** of
  the same model (owner, 2026-10-05: *"Use the nearest real year"*; equidistant takes the later year,
  derived), then the D48(4) placeholder only for a model with no real row at all, then nothing
  (`finder/fitment.ts`).
- Where a generation row disagrees with an exact row the script lists it (`import-report/`
  `vehicle-fitment-generation-review.csv`) and the exact row wins. First run: 0.

## Rules
- **`vehicles.csv` is generated. Never edit it by hand.** A wrong, missing or misnamed model is fixed
  by a rule in `vehicles-fetch.mjs` (`EXCLUDED`, `RENAMED`, `SPLIT`, `ADDED`) and a re-run, or the next
  run puts the mistake back.
- **It carries no tire size and no bolt pattern.** vPIC has neither. Fitment is keyed on this file's
  exact `make` and `model` spelling: bolt pattern and bore in `vehicle-fitment.csv` (ANKUSI; D110's
  "no open data set" held for government sources only), tire size still nowhere.
- **`vehicle-fitment.csv` is generated too.** A wrong row is fixed by a rule in `vehicle-fitment.mjs`
  (`EXCLUDED`, `ALIASES`, `TRIM_WORDS`) and a re-run, never by hand. **Never fill a year from a
  neighbouring year**: a model's bolt pattern changes between generations (Outback 5x100 → 5x114.3).
- After either CSV changes, re-run `node scripts/vehicle-fitment.mjs` so the app's two generated
  modules (`src/features/finder/vehicle-list.ts`, `vehicle-fitment-data.ts`) match.
- **The two Auto Country CSVs are generated too** (D155). Fix a wrong name decision by a row in
  `vehicle-fitment-autocountry.mjs`'s `SAME` or `FAMILY` table (each carries its reason) and re-run, never by
  editing `vehicle-aliases.csv`. Auto Country's own values are never edited: they are what the lookup said.
- **Auto Country wins** wherever it disagrees with `vehicle-fitment.csv`, the generation rows, the thread
  sheet or the wheel-size sheet (owner, 2026-10-06: *"if there is any disagreement on whatever we have already
  built from or live fitment data, then you can always put auto country data first."*). The disagreements are
  listed, not asked about, in `design/reports/fitment-overrides.csv` (one row per make + model + year + field,
  with both values and the older sheet it came from).
- **Display names (D156).** The `DISPLAY` table in `vehicle-fitment-autocountry.mjs` (eight rows, each with a
  reason line) renames Auto Country's bare model names before anything is decided: Mazda `2`/`3`/`5`/`6` ->
  `Mazda2`/`Mazda3`/`Mazda5`/`Mazda6`, Tesla `3`/`S`/`X`/`Y` -> `Model 3`/`Model S`/`Model X`/`Model Y`.
  Owner, 2026-10-06 (chose *"Familiar names (Recommended)"*): where Auto Country's name is a bare number or
  letter the maker itself markets with a prefix, the finder shows the familiar name. Maker capitals (LYRIQ,
  OPTIQ, TITAN, ESCALADE IQ) and real numeric names (Ram 1500, Chrysler 300, Fiat 500, Porsche 911, BMW 328i,
  Toyota 86) are not touched. This is the one exception to "Auto Country's name wins" (D155), and
  `vehicle-fitment-autocountry.csv`'s `model` column carries the familiar name for these eight (the raw
  answers on disk keep Auto Country's spelling). `3 Sport` folds into `Mazda3` by the `FAMILY` table. The
  eight bare spellings are `same` aliases, so `?model=3` still resolves (to `Mazda3`) and `?model=Mazda3` is
  canonical; no alias chains, none is an entry.
- `vehicle-fitment-vendors.csv` (optional, not yet created): the Auto Country columns plus `source`
  (`d` Direct Auto Import, `p` PRO WHEELS, `m` maker, `w` web). The generator only fills a field Auto Country
  left blank, or stands in for a year Auto Country lacks (marked with a trailing `1` when one source).

  `src/features/finder/vehicle-list.ts` and `vehicle-fitment-data.ts` (the finder and the results split).

## Don't
- Don't add a heavy-duty pickup back under its half-ton's name. `Silverado 1500` and
  `Silverado 2500HD` are separate rows because they do not share a bolt pattern
  (`vehicles-fetch.mjs` `SPLIT`).
- Don't treat a model's presence here as "sold in Canada". vPIC is a US register; a few rows are
  US- or Mexico-market cars that `EXCLUDED` has not caught.
